## Four-paper sequence identified

The 1922 Thompson sequence is more granular than the single 1924 Marseille paper already tracked:

1. W. R. Thompson (1922a), “Biologie—Théorie de l'action des parasites entomophages. Les formules mathématiques du parasitisme cyclique,” *Comptes rendus de l'Académie des sciences de Paris* 174, 1201–1204.
2. Thompson (1922b), “Entomologie—Étude mathématique de l'action des parasites entomophages. Durée du cycle parasitaire et accroissement de la proportion d'hôtes parasités,” same volume, 1433–1435.
3. Thompson (1922c), “Parasitologie—Étude de quelques cas simples de parasitisme cyclique chez les insectes entomophages,” same volume, 1647–1649.
4. Thompson (1922d), “Parasitisme—Théorie de l'action des parasites entomophages. Accroissement de la proportion d'hôtes parasités dans le parasitisme cyclique,” volume 175, 65–68.

The sequence is corroborated by the Cambridge *Parasitology* bibliography for W. R. Thompson, by a specialist pooled bibliography, and by an independent bibliography in an open Oxford repository thesis. These are bibliographic corroborations, not equation transcriptions.

## Relationship to the 1924 Marseille paper

The 1924 paper, “La théorie mathématique de l'action des parasites entomophages et le facteur du hasard,” *Annales de la Faculté des Sciences de Marseille* 2, 69–89, is a later and apparently broader treatment. The 1922 notes may contain earlier forms, derivations or special cases. Do not count each title as a distinct model or statistical unit without tracing the equation lineage and assumptions across the notes and 1924 paper.

A separate contemporary/secondary bibliography lists the 1923 *Revue générale des sciences pures et appliquées* treatment (11 pages), which may serve as an intermediate synthesis. It too is a retrieval lead, not an equation source.

## Retrieval status and limitations

Searches located reliable bibliographic citations but did not locate an equation-bearing primary scan or full text for the four short *Comptes rendus* notes. No formula is transcribed. The specialist bibliography's sequence is useful for targeted archive searches in Gallica / Académie des sciences / Comptes rendus historical archives, but it cannot establish exact notation, recurrence order, host/parasitoid state definitions, or the random-allocation assumption.

## Structural-family screening questions

Before any candidate is promoted:
- Determine whether the four notes are successive derivations of one cyclic-parasitism process, distinct timing conventions, or separate special cases.
- Compare the 1922 equations against Thompson (1924), Bailey (1931, 1933), Lotka (1923), Nicholson–Bailey (1935), and the random-egg allocation formulation later reproduced by Royama (1971).
- Distinguish host depletion / random egg allocation / parasitoid emergence / generation delay mechanisms. Do not collapse them into a generic “host–parasite recurrence”.
- Keep author/source-family identity separate from statistical independence unit.
